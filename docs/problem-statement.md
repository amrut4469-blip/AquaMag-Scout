# 📌 Problem Statement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Problem Statement ID | SIH26064 |
| Title | Low-Cost Deployable Seafloor Metal Detection Sensor for Ocean Resource Exploration |
| Ministry | Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) |
| Theme | Robotics and Drones |
| Category | Hardware |

## Background
The seabed holds metal-bearing features such as polymetallic nodules,
sulphide deposits and metal-rich crusts. Finding them early and cheaply is
the first step of any ocean resource programme, including India's Deep Ocean
Mission.

## Core Challenges
| Challenge | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Dark, deep, remote seabed | No visibility; operators depend fully on sensors and vehicles |
| Costly, heavy survey sensors | Limits how many vehicles and surveys can be afforded |
| Slow and sparse exploration | Large areas stay unscreened; sampling is done blindly |

## Objective
Design a low-cost, compact sensor that can be mounted on an ROV, AUV or
lander and flags metal-rich spots on the seabed.

## Scope
- Detect metal anomalies using fused magnetic and electromagnetic sensing
- Classify the anomaly type: Nodule / Sulphide / Crust
- Geo-tag detections and present them as a heatmap

## Out of Scope
- Estimating resource quantity or grade
- Mining or sampling hardware
- Vehicle design (the pod is designed to fit existing vehicles)

## Success Criteria (to be measured)
- Detects known metal targets in a water-tank trial
- False-alarm behaviour measured and reported honestly
- Fits on a standard ROV / towfish without vehicle modification
- Bill of materials stays well below conventional survey sensors
